Chapter XXXIII

Phrases

Six kinds of Phrases

(1) Noun Phrase — does the work of a Noun.
যে Phrase বাক্যে Noun-এর কাজ করে, তাকে Noun Phrase বলে।
  • To ride well requires practice.
  • He likes to play tennis.
  • He enjoys walking by the river-side.
(2) Adjective Phrase — does the work of an Adjective.
যে Phrase কোনো Noun/Pronoun-কে বিশেষিত করে।
  • He gave me a chain of gold (= a gold chain).
  • He is without fear (= fearless).
  • The man in the street knows it.
Note — The same phrase may be Noun or Adjectival depending on use.
(3) Adverbial Phrase — does the work of an Adverb.
যে Phrase ক্রিয়াকে বিশেষিত করে।
  • He worked with care (= carefully).
  • He came at a great speed (= quickly).
  • This must be done at any price.
(4) Prepositional Phrase — does the work of a Preposition.
একাধিক শব্দ মিলে Preposition-এর কাজ করে।
  • He stood in front of me.
  • He could not come on account of illness.
(5) Conjunctional Phrase — does the work of a Conjunction.
  • Come as soon as you can.
  • Not only he but his brother also were present.
(6) Interjectional Phrase — does the work of an Interjection.
  • What a pity! · By Jove! · Good heavens!
Chapter XXXIV

Clauses

What is a Clause?

A Clause is a sentence forming part of a bigger sentence — a group of words having a subject and a finite verb of its own.
একগুচ্ছ শব্দ যার নিজস্ব Subject ও Finite Verb আছে, কিন্তু যা একটি বৃহত্তর বাক্যের অংশ।

Remember: the number of clauses equals the number of finite verbs.

Kinds of Clauses

Principal Clause — stands by itself and makes complete sense.
একা দাঁড়িয়ে সম্পূর্ণ অর্থ প্রকাশ করে।
Subordinate / Dependent Clause — depends on another clause.
অন্য Clause-এর উপর নির্ভরশীল।
Co-ordinate Clause — same rank, joined by and, but, or, yet, etc.
সমান মর্যাদার Clause, Co-ordinating Conjunction দিয়ে যুক্ত।

A. The Noun Clause

Does the work of a Noun. Introduced by that, what, who, why, when, if/whether.
Noun-এর কাজ করে — Subject, Object, Complement বা Apposition হিসেবে।
  • I know that he is ill. (object)
  • That he is ill is known to all. (subject)
  • I know nothing of what he will do. (object of preposition)

B. The Adjective Clause

Does the work of an Adjective, qualifying a noun or pronoun.
Noun/Pronoun-কে বিশেষিত করে।
  • I know the boy who did it.
  • This is the place where he was born.
Restrictive (defines) = Subordinate Adjectival. Continuative (extra detail = and he/it) = Co-ordinate.

C. The Adverbial Clause

Does the work of an Adverb.
ক্রিয়া/বিশেষণকে বিশেষিত করে।
Nine kinds of Adverbial Clause
KindIntroduced byExample
Timewhen, while, after, before, till, sinceHe came when I was there.
Placewhere, whereverStay where you are.
Reasonbecause, as, sinceHe cannot come because he is ill.
Purposethat, so that, lestWe read that we may learn.
Resultso…that, such…thatI am so tired that I cannot walk.
MannerasIt happened as I expected.
Conditionif, unless, providedI shall not go unless you come.
Concessionthough, although, even ifThough he is poor, he is honest.
Comparisonas…as, than, the…theHe is not so tall as his brother.

Function decides the kind

  • I know where he lives — Noun clause
  • I know the place where he lives — Adjective clause
  • I shall go where he lives — Adverbial clause
Chapter XXXV

Simple, Complex, Compound Sentences

Simple Sentence

Only one subject and one finite verb.
মাত্র একটি Subject ও একটি Finite Verb।
  • I came home yesterday.
  • Barking dogs seldom bite.

Complex Sentence

One Principal Clause + one or more Subordinate Clauses.
একটি Principal + এক বা একাধিক Subordinate Clause।
Worked example — As you are rich, you must help those who are poor.
  1. As you are rich — Subordinate Adverbial
  2. you must help those — Principal
  3. who are poor — Subordinate Adjective

Compound Sentence

Two or more independent Principal clauses joined by co-ordinating conjunctions.
দুই বা ততোধিক Principal Clause, Co-ordinating Conjunction দিয়ে যুক্ত।

Complex or Compound? The test

Depends on the number of Principal clauses. Independent co-ordinates → Compound; subordinate → Complex.

Chapters XXXVI – XXXVII

Analysis of Sentences

The Parts of a Sentence

GroupParts
Subject groupSubject · Adjuncts to Subject
Predicate groupPredicate-verb · Object · Complements · Adverbial Adjuncts

Analysing a Simple Sentence

  1. Find the Finite Verb → Predicate
  2. Find the Subject
  3. Note Adjuncts of the Subject
  4. Note Object(s) if transitive
  5. Note Complement(s)
  6. Note Adverbial Adjuncts

Analysing a Complex Sentence

  1. Divide into as many clauses as finite verbs
  2. Find the Principal clause
  3. Classify each subordinate (Noun / Adjective / Adverbial)
  4. Show relations

Analysing a Compound Sentence

  1. Break into clauses by finite verbs
  2. Identify principal co-ordinate clauses
  3. Identify any subordinate clauses and relations
Chapter XXXVIII

A Few Difficulties in Analysis

Quoted Sentences

A quotation is not a Noun Clause. Treat it as a noun-equivalent object of the reporting verb — so He said, "I am ill" is Simple.

Parenthetic Expressions

In "He is the man who, I believe, did it," — I believe is a Parenthetic Clause, not Principal or Subordinate.

The Use of 'It'

  • It … that — relative vs conjunction in apposition
  • It + infinitive — formal subject; infinitive is real subject
  • It as formal object — infinitive/clause in apposition

Ellipses

Always supply what is understood. He is stronger than I → Than I (am strong).

Chapter XXXIX

Syntax

Subject–Verb Agreement

A verb must agree with its subject in number and person.
Verb-কে Subject-এর Number ও Person অনুযায়ী মিলতে হবে।
  • I am happy.
  • They were happy.
  • All is well.
Common pitfall — verb wrongly made to agree with the predicate noun instead of the true subject.
